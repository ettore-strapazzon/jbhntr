{# ======================= HOW IT WORKS ======================= #}

How it works

1. Upload your CV and five minutes

Upload your CV, say where you will work, and describe what you are after in your own words. That is the whole setup. Add a second CV, an old cover letter, the roles you do not want, and everything after this gets sharper.

2. The market gets read while you do something else

One search pulls from 25+ sources: the big engines, niche remote boards, and companies' own career pages, where roles appear first. Postings are filtered to your locations, ranked by meaning rather than keywords, then scored. Takes a minute or two.

3. Read the ones worth reading

Every role gets two scores, how well it fits what you want and how well you fit what they asked for, plus a plain case for and against. Sorted into apply now, strong, possible and long shots, so you know where the evening goes.

{# =============== WHY IT BEATS A JOB ALERT (S-06 band) =============== #}

Job alerts send you everything. This sends the few worth reading.

  • Matching works on meaning. A role called "Backend Developer" still reaches you when you searched for "Software Engineer", and one called "Product Manager" will not, if it is really a project management job.
  • The requirements get read too. Most tools say a job matches your search. Here you also see where you fall short of it, before you spend an hour on the application.
  • Career pages are watched directly. Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and the rest, where roles are posted days before they reach the aggregators, and sometimes never do.
  • Your ratings change what comes back. Rate a match out of five, add a line about why, and the next search weighs it. Twenty ratings is enough to change what comes back.
