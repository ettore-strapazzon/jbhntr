{# ======================= HOW IT WORKS (R3.4) ======================= #}

How it works

1. Give it your CV and five minutes

Upload your CV, say where you will work, and describe what you are after in your own words. That is the whole setup. The more you tell it, a second CV, an old cover letter, the things you do not want, the sharper everything after this gets.

2. It reads the market while you do something else

One search pulls from 25+ sources: the big engines, niche remote boards, and companies' own career pages, where roles appear first. It filters to your locations, ranks by meaning rather than keywords, then scores what survives. Takes a minute or two.

3. Read the ones worth reading

Every role gets two scores, how well it fits what you want and how well you fit what they asked for, plus a plain case for and against. Sorted into apply now, strong, possible and long shots, so you know where the evening goes.

{# ==================== WHY IT BEATS A JOB ALERT ==================== #}

Job alerts send you everything. This sends you the few things worth reading.

  • It matches on meaning. A role called "Backend Developer" still reaches you when you searched for "Software Engineer", and one called "Product Manager" will not, if it is really a project management job.
  • It reads the requirements too. Most tools tell you a job matches your search. This one also tells you where you fall short of it, before you spend an hour on the application.
  • It watches career pages directly. Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and the rest, where roles are posted days before they reach the aggregators, and sometimes never do.
  • It gets better because you correct it. Rate a match out of five, add a line about why, and the next search weighs it. Twenty ratings is enough to change what comes back.
{# ==================== TAILORED APPLICATIONS ==================== #}

When you find one, it writes the first draft.

One click on a role gives you a CV rewritten for that specific posting and a cover letter in your own register, because it learned that from the ones you wrote. Download, edit, send. It is a first draft, so read it before you send it.
