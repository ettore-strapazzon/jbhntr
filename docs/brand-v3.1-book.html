v2 said what the brand is. v3 makes it buildable.

The strategy, voice, wordlist and claim governance in v2 were strong and are carried forward with minimal edits. What was missing was everything a designer or engineer needs to produce a correct asset without asking a question: token values, component anatomy, state matrices, per-platform specifications and a real image library. The honest note

A brand book is judged on its specimens, not its prose. v2 ran to thirty-three pages with roughly six images, most of them a single concept board carrying a whole chapter. Anyone handed that document would still have to invent the button, the card, the dark surface and the crop. v3 fixes the ratio: every rule that can be shown is shown, and every value an engineer would otherwise guess is written down.

Still outstanding, and not inventable here: commissioned photography, founder facts, real product screenshots, trademark clearance and an accessibility audit. Their placeholders are marked throughout.

01 · Strategic foundation

Decide first. Apply second.

JBHNTR helps professionals decide which roles are worth pursuing by assessing both the opportunity against their preferences and their experience against the role. Application support follows that decision. This order is the core of the brand.

Purpose
Help people spend their job-search effort where it matters.
Mission
Turn a fragmented market into a considered shortlist, with reasons and practical next steps.
Vision
A labour market in which professionals and companies connect through a clearer understanding of mutual fit.
Promise
A clearer view of your options and the evidence behind each match.

The brand is precise, composed and human. It should feel like a capable research partner who has read the details and respects the candidate's judgment. People are never reduced to their scores. A role can be unsuitable without the person being inadequate.

Keep three tensions productive: breadth of discovery with a selective output; intelligent automation with human decisions; candidate usefulness with viable employer monetisation. Successful search can end active usage — return visits, referrals and relevant passive alerts still create long-term value.

Never claim
To be the only matching platform · to cover every live role · to guarantee interviews · to possess a proven outcome-data moat. Each requires evidence beyond the deck.
Brand story

A professional does not choose a role on experience alone. They weigh the work, the people, the location and what a move could mean for their life. Yet the search process often turns those judgments into a long sequence of tabs, titles and repeated applications.

JBHNTR starts with the person and their priorities. It reads opportunities against that context and assesses the advertised requirements against the person's experience. The result should be a shortlist with reasons to consider each role and points to check.

Our identity reflects the meeting of those two perspectives. Its paired forms belong together without becoming identical. Warm colour and real work settings acknowledge the person behind the profile. Clear type and restrained product surfaces make the information usable. This is the brand story, not a fabricated account of the company's founding.

Mechanics

Second person for candidates. Sentence case, active verbs, short paragraphs, one clear action per message. Explain AI when it helps someone interpret a result. Avoid calling every routine feature intelligent or revolutionary. Never mimic recruitment rejection language in product feedback.

Global English master uses British spelling: labour, personalised, optimise. US editions use labor and resume consistently with the market. 05 · Commercial communication and sharing

Paid placement is allowed. Hiding it is not.

Sponsored placements are an available business-model option. The brand does not make a public commitment to excluding employer payments from placement or visibility. Paid visibility and match assessment must be understandable to the user.

Sponsored-card standard
Show “Sponsored” in visible text on the card and retain it in detail view.
Identify the advertiser by name.
Place “Why am I seeing this?” close to the label, explaining that payment affects placement.
Never present a purchased position as evidence of a higher fit score.
A sponsored role may appear in a dedicated module or a labelled slot within results. Frequency and eligibility rules belong to product experiments.

Feature dependencies

Declining recruiter discoverability naturally means no recruiter discovery through that feature. Credits

06 · Logo system

Two forms that belong together without becoming identical.

The symbol is two rounded forms on a 100-unit square, and the meaning lives in where they cross. Navy is the candidate — experience and priorities. Cornflower is the role — requirements and context. The apricot lens where they overlap is the match: the common ground the whole product exists to find. Both forms must stay legible on their own, and the overlap must always read as a third, distinct shape — never as a shadow, never merged into one block. The wordmark is an outlined Plus Jakarta Sans ExtraBold construction; typing the name in the font is not a substitute for the asset.

13 · Iconography

One family, one stroke, always labelled.

19 · Photography direction

People deciding, not people celebrating.

Every JBHNTR photograph shows a believable professional in a real work context, at a moment of evaluation rather than triumph. Warmth comes from the person and the light, never from a filter. If a picture could sit unchanged in any recruitment advertisement, it is the wrong picture.

Always
Natural daylight, directional but soft. Visible shadow is allowed.
Varied ages, roles, body types, mobility and settings — without making seniority look like one gender, ethnicity or clothing style.
Tools of thought in frame: notes, a screen turned away, a printed posting, coffee going cold.
Eye-line off camera. The subject is thinking, not presenting.
Room to crop: shoot wide enough to deliver 16:9, 4:5 and 1:1 from one frame.
Navy, warm neutral and denim clothing tones; nothing that competes with apricot.
Never
Handshakes, high-fives, fist bumps, applauding meeting rooms.
A wall of smiling headshots used as a decorative grid.
AI stars, robots, glowing gradients, glass panels, floating UI chrome.
Legible motivational posters or branded laptop stickers.
Heavy colour grading, teal-and-orange, vignettes or artificial grain.
Implying a stock model is a customer, a founder or an employee.
Crop and composition
Rectangular crops only — radius 0 on editorial photography. The hero diptych aligns two portrait crops to one common outer rectangle with a 2–4 px seam of warm white. Subject sits off-centre with negative space on the text side. Never crop through a hand or at a joint.
Grading
Neutral white balance, slightly warm. Highlights held, blacks lifted marginally so shadow detail survives print. Saturation at capture level. No LUT that pushes skin toward orange. Deliver both the original and the graded file.
Text over image
Type sits on a solid plate, not on the photograph. Where an overlay is unavoidable, use a navy scrim at 70% or more and re-measure contrast against the lightest pixel under the text — not the average.

v3.1 · 01 · Changelog

What v3.1 changes, and why.

Five moves. Each raises restraint and precision — never saturation. The instant JBHNTR looks loud, the honest-broker position cracks. Premium here means considered, not decorated.

01
Layered depth. Elevation is now three stacked, warm navy-tinted shadows so light falls off gradually. The single biggest "expensive" cue, at zero cost.
02
A lit ground. The warm-white canvas carries two very faint tints — cornflower and apricot — so the page reads as a surface, not a flat fill.
03
Dimensional controls. Primary buttons gain a 1px inner top-highlight and a hover lift; flat controls stay flat. Tactile, not plastic.
04
The verdict. A new first-class brand object: the two-way judgement — Apply now / Worth a stretch / Watch out — now leads the match card.
05
Apricot earns its job. The warm accent maps to one idea only — judgement — and now owns the strongest verdict.
v3.1 · 02 · Surfaces

Depth, not saturation.

The uplift is material, not chromatic. Warm-tinted layered shadows, hairlines in the paper's own hue, and a barely-there lit ground. You feel the depth before you notice it.

Elevation — raised
--elev-1: 0 1px 1px rgba(23,51,75,.05), 0 2px 4px rgba(23,51,75,.05), 0 8px 16px rgba(23,51,75,.05); --elev-2: 0 1px 2px rgba(23,51,75,.06), 0 6px 12px rgba(23,51,75,.08), 0 20px 40px -8px rgba(23,51,75,.14);

Three layers, not one. The tint is navy, never grey — grey shadow on warm paper reads muddy.

The lit ground
background: radial-gradient(1100px 620px at 12% -10%, color-mix(in srgb,#7599DB 7%,transparent), transparent 60%), radial-gradient(950px 520px at 100% 0%, color-mix(in srgb,#E8A67E 6%,transparent), transparent 55%), #FBF8F2; background-attachment: fixed;

Under 8% each. Full-bleed bands paint over it; content columns let it whisper through.

Do

  • Warm-tinted, multi-layer shadow for depth.
  • Hairlines in the paper's hue (#E4DED2), not grey.
  • Keep tints faint enough to doubt they're there.

Never

  • Glassmorphism, blur panels, or glow.
  • Animated or high-contrast gradient backgrounds.
  • A fourth colour. Premium is precision with the three we have.
v3.1 · 03 · Controls

Dimensional, not plastic.

A 1px top highlight makes the solid fill read as a lit surface; a soft elevation on hover lifts it a hair. Reserved for primary actions — flat controls (ghost, link, chips, toggles) opt out so the hierarchy stays legible.
